# Kanojo Magazine — Content Tone Design

**Date:** 2026-03-08
**Status:** Approved

## Target Audience

Both potential new customers and existing users. The magazine serves discovery (brand introduction) and depth (ongoing engagement) simultaneously.

## Reader Experience

Every article should leave the reader feeling:
- **Inspired** — "I want to create something beautiful like this"
- **Informed** — "Now I understand something I didn't before"

## Editorial Voice

**Core character:** Quietly authoritative. Kanojo Magazine writes like a person who has thought deeply about beauty, light, and the way images make us feel — and shares that thinking without hurry.

The voice is:
- **Unhurried** — long sentences are allowed. Paragraphs breathe.
- **Observational** — describes what something *feels like*, not just what it *is*
- **Restrained** — never uses exclamation marks. Never oversells.
- **Precise** — chooses one exact word over three approximate ones

**Reference aesthetic:** Kinfolk / Cereal — quiet, considered, slow aesthetics.

**Language:** English only.

### What to avoid

- Marketing language ("powerful", "amazing", "game-changing")
- Second-person commands ("Try this!", "You should...")
- Listicle energy (no "5 tips for...")
- Technical jargon without context

### Tone examples

Wrong:
> Create stunning AI portraits with our powerful new face engine! Here's how to get the best results.

Right:
> There is something about a face caught in soft window light that resists explanation. The camera sees it. So does the eye. What draws us to certain portraits, and what can we learn from that pull?

## Content Philosophy

Kanojo Magazine covers **culture & aesthetics** — filtered through the act of *seeing*: how we look at people, how light shapes meaning, how beauty is constructed and perceived.

### Topics that fit

- The history or cultural meaning of a photographic style (soft focus, chiaroscuro, natural light portraiture)
- Essays on beauty as visual language — what a certain makeup era communicated, what a pose says
- Seasonal or atmospheric mood studies — how winter light differs from summer light, and what that does to a portrait
- The aesthetics of a place, a fabric, a color
- Quiet profiles of visual artists, photographers, directors who influence the platform's aesthetic

### Topics that don't fit

- News, trends for trend's sake, product announcements written as articles
- Tutorials or how-to guides (those belong elsewhere in the app)
- Anything that requires urgency or timeliness to be interesting

### The underlying question

Every article should quietly work toward answering:

> *Why does this image, this light, this face move us?*

## Article Structure & Length

**Length:** 600–1,000 words. Long enough to develop an idea with depth. Short enough to read in one sitting.

**Structure (loose, not rigid):**

1. **Opening image or observation** — a specific scene, a detail, a question. Not a thesis statement.
2. **The idea unfolds** — expand outward from that opening. Bring in context: history, aesthetics, cultural meaning.
3. **A moment of stillness** — one paragraph that slows down and sits with something. The emotional core.
4. **A quiet close** — end on an image or thought, not a conclusion. Leave the reader with something to carry.

**No subheadings.** Articles flow as prose, not sections. The reader follows the thinking.

**Cover images:** Every article requires one strong, mood-setting image — a portrait, a scene, a texture. Sets the emotional register before the first word is read.

**Cadence:** One article per week. Quality over volume. An archive of 50 considered articles is worth more than 200 rushed ones.
